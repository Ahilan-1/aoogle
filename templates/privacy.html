Privacy policy

Exactly what data we collect, what we don't, and why you can trust it.

arlong is a privacy-first search engine. Our design philosophy is simple: if we don't collect the data, we can't lose it, sell it, or be forced to hand it over. This page documents every piece of data that does - and does not - touch our systems.

Short version: We collect almost nothing. No IPs, no user agents, no search histories, no cookies, no fingerprints. You are a ghost to us, and we intend to keep it that way.

What we collect

The only data we persist is aggregate, anonymized statistics used for our live dashboard. No individual search can be identified, linked, or reconstructed.

What "no logging" really means

Most search engines claim they "anonymize" your data after a period of time. That means they do collect it first, then try to strip identifiers later. That is not what we do.

No logging means the data never exists in the first place.

Here is the technical reality of how our no-logging policy works at every layer of the stack:

Application layer (Python / Flask)

Our Flask application does not call logging.info() or any equivalent for incoming requests. The standard Flask request logging that most websites enable by default is explicitly disabled. Search queries are passed directly to upstream providers in memory and never touch a write buffer, database connection, or file handle.

Web server layer (Gunicorn / Vercel)

We run with access logging disabled. The web server is configured to never write access logs. This means no raw request lines, no status codes paired with IPs, no user-agent strings persisted at the infrastructure level. If your hosting provider or load balancer generates logs (e.g., Vercel's edge logs), those logs contain only ephemeral metadata with no query content and are subject to their infrastructure policies - we do not read, store, or export them.

Database layer

There is no database of searches. Our data storage (S3 and local JSON files) contains only:

  • Search result cache (URLs, titles, snippets - public data from search engines)
  • Aggregate hourly stats counters (a single integer per hour with no link to any request)
  • Domain blacklists and user-submitted reports (manually curated, no personal data)

There is no table, collection, or file that maps a search query to a user, IP, or time.

Upstream requests

When you search, arlong fetches results from DuckDuckGo and Bing on your behalf. We do not add tracking parameters, unique identifiers, or custom headers to these requests. The upstream providers see a request from our server, not from you. We use a rotating pool of user-agent strings to blend in with regular traffic, further protecting your anonymity.

How we collect stats data

The only persistent data we collect is the hourly search count displayed on our live stats page. Here is exactly how that works:

  • What is stored: A single JSON object mapping hour-of-day (0 through 23) to a count. For example: {"14": 47, "15": 53} means 47 searches were made during the 14:00 hour and 53 during the 15:00 hour.
  • How it is incremented: Every time the /search endpoint is called, an in-memory counter for the current hour is incremented by one. That counter is periodically flushed to disk/S3.
  • What it does NOT store: No query text, no IP, no user-agent, no timestamp, no session ID, no referrer. The counter is a bare integer with zero context.
  • Granularity: Per-minute counters exist in memory for the real-time dashboard but are aggregated to hourly before persistence. Per-minute data is lost on server restart and is never written to disk.
  • Why we collect it: To show users that the search engine is active and to help us understand traffic patterns for capacity planning. No personal data is needed for this.

Can the stats data identify you? No. A count of "47 searches at 14:00" cannot be reverse-engineered to know what was searched, who searched it, or even whether the same person searched twice. It is a number, nothing more.

What data we explicitly NEVER collect

To be absolutely clear, here is a comprehensive list of data that never enters our systems:

  • Search queries or search history
  • IP addresses (IPv4 or IPv6) — see community section below for the sole exception
  • Browser user-agent strings
  • Device fingerprints or canvas fingerprints
  • Session or persistent cookies (for regular visitors)
  • Referring URLs or clickstream data
  • Geolocation data (IP geolocation is never stored or logged)
  • Browser language or timezone settings
  • Screen resolution or color depth
  • Installed fonts or browser plugins
  • Touchscreen or hardware capabilities
  • Advertising IDs or device IDs
  • Email addresses (unless you email us directly for support)
  • Full card or bank details (subscription payments are collected and processed by Dodo Payments; we retain billing identifiers, plan, subscription status, renewal dates, and usage allowances)
  • Social media profiles or connections
  • Contact lists or address books
  • Any biometric or health data
  • Any data from cookies set by third parties

Advertising on the homepage

arlong has introduced optional banner advertisements on the homepage portal only. Here is what you need to know:

  • No tracking. Advertisements are static image banners. No tracking pixels, no cookies, no fingerprinting.
  • No search result ads. Ads never appear in search results — only on the homepage portal.
  • Rental model. Advertisers rent banner space for a fixed duration (6 hours to 10 days). There is no bidding, no auction, no real-time ad exchange.
  • No personal data shared. We do not share any user data with advertisers. Ads are displayed based solely on a first-come, first-served queue.
  • Admin curated. Every advertisement is manually reviewed before it goes live.

Your privacy is unchanged. The introduction of homepage ads does not affect our data collection practices. We still collect nothing — no IPs, no queries, no browsing history. See our Advertising policy for full details.

Legal compliance

We designed arlong to make compliance with legal data requests technically impossible because we do not possess the data to hand over.

  • No search history to subpoena. We simply do not have it.
  • No IP logs to monitor. They never existed.
  • No user accounts to de-anonymize. Accounts are optional and used only for community features (voting, comments, domain reports). An account does not unlock personal data collection — we still collect nothing beyond what you actively submit.
  • No personal data to export. GDPR Article 20 data portability requests are trivially fulfilled: there is no data to port.
  • No data to delete. GDPR Article 17 right to erasure is trivially fulfilled: there is nothing to erase.

If we ever receive a legal request (court order, subpoena, national security letter), we will challenge it to the fullest extent possible. Because we designed the system to log nothing, we can honestly certify that there is no responsive data to produce.

Changes to this policy

If we ever change how data is collected (which is unlikely, given our design principles), we will update this page and note the date of the last change. We will never retroactively change how your data is treated without explicit notice.

Community system (votes, comments, domain reports)

arlong has an optional community system that lets signed-in users upvote, downvote, and comment on search results, as well as report low-quality domains. Here is exactly what data is involved:

  • Account creation: You choose a username and password. No email is required. Your password is salted and hashed — we never store it in plain text. You also provide a security question and answer for account recovery, which is similarly hashed.
  • IP address: When you perform community actions (vote, comment, report), your IP address is hashed with SHA-256 and stored as a partial hash (first 16 hex characters) to prevent spam and abuse. The full IP is never stored or logged. IP hashes are used only to detect duplicate accounts from the same source.
  • Votes and comments: Your username, vote (+1/−1), and comment text are stored alongside the URL they apply to. This data is public — anyone viewing a search result can see the vote score and comments. Your user ID is internal and never exposed.
  • Domain reports: When you report a domain, your user ID is recorded to prevent duplicate reports. Domain reports are reviewed by moderators and may result in the domain being blacklisted.
  • Rate limiting: To prevent spam, there is an 11-second initial wait and a 70-second gap between community actions. Your last action time is stored to enforce this.

Your privacy is still protected. No email, no real name, no tracking across the web. The only data we keep is what is strictly necessary for the community system to function and prevent abuse. You can vote and comment without revealing anything about who you are.
